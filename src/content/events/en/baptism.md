The church held maybe thirty people, light falling in long bars through narrow windows onto worn stone floors. It was a small gathering — close family, a handful of friends — and the whole service had the unhurried quality of a morning with nowhere else to be.

I stayed at the edges, moving only when the light or the moment asked for it: a grandmother's hand resting on a small shoulder, the priest's quiet instructions, the particular stillness just before the water. Nothing was arranged for the camera, which is exactly how I prefer to work.

Afterward, everyone spilled out onto the church steps into full sun, and the day opened up into something looser and louder — plates passed around a long table, a toast, a lot of laughing. A small occasion, photographed with the attention it deserved.
